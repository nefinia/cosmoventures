Is this you?

  • You want a second opinion from someone trained in another field.
  • You are about to invest in a space or deeptech company and need someone independent to check the science.
  • Your company builds instruments, satellites or data products, and clients and investors will test the physics behind them.
  • Your AI models are used for science or engineering, and you need to know where their reasoning fails.
  • Your strategy or innovation team has to judge forecasts and roadmaps, and has no way to score them.
  • Your agency, observatory or research organisation has a programme budget and wants the public to understand what you do.

Why a view from outside

More experts from the same field tend to miss the same things. In an independent study replaying a real AI incident, four reviewers with different roles caught more than four identical ones, and every set-up still shared one blind spot. The useful second opinion is the one that was trained differently.

Sofia Gallego was trained as an astrophysicist on the faint gas between galaxies, and has spent as long making things outside science: games, music, workshops, stories. Each of those is a different way of reading a problem. Put together, they tend to surface the question a project didn't know it had.

That is what runs through the work here. A radio observatory explained through what its instruments actually hear. Stellar evolution turned into a strategy game. Science fiction read as a dataset of predictions and scored. A workshop on innovation that ends with the room running an experiment on itself.

About

Sofia Gallego is an extragalactic astrophysicist. Her research at ETH Zurich, Caltech and Université Paris Cité followed the faint gas between and around galaxies: filaments of the cosmic web seen in Lyman-alpha emission, a protocluster observed with KCWI and HST, outflows and intracluster light in dense environments.

CosmoVentures carries that scientific training into consulting, workshops, games and independent research on AI safety. Alongside it runs a long-term project on astronomy and Indigenous cosmovisions, rooted in her Chilean origins, and a practice in writing and music. She works in English, French, Spanish, Italian and German.
