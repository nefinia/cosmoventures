Perceptual Rendering

Adaptive reading, shown through small interactive experiments.

A proof of concept for adaptive reading: the same text rendered in different ways for different readers. It shows how learning products can be tested with small, measurable experiments.

  • Overview
  • Pig feeding experiment
  • Topic reader

Same information, different renderings

The claim is modest and testable: people often struggle less with the content than with the presentation. The prototype lets a reader switch how text is structured (the mode bar) and tune readability (the display controls), while the underlying content stays the same.

What this is

  • A minimal demo of a rendering layer: structure and formatting adapt to the reader.
  • No AI, no personality typing, no claims about fixed learning styles.
  • The aim is to see whether certain renderings reduce friction and improve comprehension and recall.

How to use it

  1. Pick a topic below.
  2. Switch rendering modes in the mode bar: Skimmer, Stepper, Deep Reader, Visual Chunker or Socratic.
  3. Adjust the comfort controls in the left panel: font, size, spacing and line width.
  4. Notice which mode makes things click faster, with less fatigue.
